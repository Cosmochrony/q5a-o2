  \title{Fourier Structure of the Pre-Saturation Admissible Pipeline:\\
  What the Three-Coordinate Projection Does and Does Not Measure}
  \author{J\'er\^ome Beau}
  \date{2026}
  \maketitle

  \begin{abstract}
    The numerical pipeline of the spectral admissibility programme stores, for each
    conjugate Weil pair $(c, q-c)$, two distinct objects: a full Gram--Schmidt basis of
    $\C^q$ reaching rank $q$ at saturation, and a three-coordinate array of projections
    onto the first three selected basis vectors.
    This paper establishes the exact Fourier structure of the pre-saturation pipeline and
    the epistemic status of the three-coordinate reduction.
    The positive results are analytic: every BFS fingerprint evaluated at the uniform
    initial vector is a pure Fourier mode, with frequency given by the O12 displacement
    formula, and the Gram--Schmidt procedure preserves this purity, so every selected
    basis vector of the pre-saturation window is itself a pure Fourier mode.
    In the pre-saturation regime $n_*(q) = o(q)$ the realised displacements satisfy
    $|b_j| \le n_*(q) = o(q)$; for block parameters held fixed independently of $q$ the
    realised frequencies are $o(q)$, while the implemented pipeline samples the block
    parameters uniformly modulo $q$, so per-$q$ frequencies of macroscopic size occur and
    no $q$-independent limit frequency is defined by the block structure in either regime.
    The delimiting results follow.
    First, the three-coordinate array is a truncation fixed by the pipeline constant
    \texttt{HEFF\_DIM = 3}; every quantity computed from it lives, by construction, in the
    span of the first three selected vectors, so no such computation can measure the rank
    of the admissible sector or exclude further directions.
    Second, a uniform coercivity bound $\Eq(f,f) \ge c\,q^{-1}\|f\|^2$ is ruled out: the
    admissibility form is $O(q^{-2})$ on the whole unit sphere of $\Cq$, a direct
    consequence of its $q^{-2}$ normalisation prefactor, consistent with the exact
    identification of the canonical filtration as a growing Fourier window
    $\Omega_n$ of dimension $\min(2n+1, q)$ whose published admissibility form converges
    to the zero form~\cite{Beau2026q5a}; the spectral-gap question for the unnormalised
    form is untouched by this statement.
    The hypotheses [H-$\mathcal{E}$1] and [C] of the original Q5a framework are therefore
    not closed by the finite-rank route; their disposition on the canonical filtration is
    the one established in~\cite{Beau2026q5a}.
  \end{abstract}

  \paragraph{Keywords.}
    Weil representation, Heisenberg group, Fourier modes, Gram--Schmidt pipeline,

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:intro}

  The paper~\cite{Beau2026q5a} (Q5a) studies the large-$q$ limit of the admissible
  sector of the Weil representation of $\Heis(\Z/q\Z)$.
  Its original framework isolated two analytic hypotheses:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{[H-$\mathcal{E}$1]:} uniform Poincar\'e inequality for the
    admissible Dirichlet form $\Eq$;
    \item \textbf{[C]:} spectral tightness implying Mosco convergence
    $\Eq \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Mosco}} \mathcal{E}$.
  \end{itemize}
  The current version of Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a} identifies the canonical filtration of
  the admissible fibre exactly: with the pipeline's uniform initial vector, the stage
  $\Omega_n$ is the toric Fourier window
  $\mathrm{span}\{e_b : |b| \le n\}$, of dimension $\min(2n+1, q)$, and the published
  admissibility form converges to the zero form on this filtration, uniformly on
  norm-bounded subsets; its Mosco limit is the zero form.

  The present paper addresses the complementary, pipeline-level questions: what exactly
  the numerical objects of the O-series pipeline~\cite{Beau2026a29} measure in the
  pre-saturation regime, and which analytic conclusions can and cannot be drawn from
  them.
  The positive part is an exact Fourier description of the pre-saturation pipeline.
  The delimiting part establishes that the widely used three-coordinate reduction is a
  truncation whose outputs cannot certify the rank of the admissible sector, and that the
  scaled coercivity bound conjectured in the original framework is ruled out on the whole
  unit sphere, as a consequence of the form's normalisation prefactor.

  \paragraph{Organisation.}
    Section~\ref{sec:setup} recalls the setup and distinguishes the two projection
    objects present in the numerical pipeline.
    Section~\ref{sec:purity} proves the pure-mode structure of the pre-saturation
    pipeline and describes the structure of the realised frequencies.
    Section~\ref{sec:truncation} establishes the truncation status of the
    three-coordinate reduction.
    Section~\ref{sec:coercivity} rules out the $q^{-1}$ coercivity scale.
    Section~\ref{sec:update} records the resulting status of the Q5a hypotheses.

  \section{Setup and two projection objects}
  \label{sec:setup}

  \subsection{The admissible Dirichlet form}

    Let $q$ be a prime, $G_q = \Heis(\Z/q\Z)$ with generators
    $S_q = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$, and $\rho_c$ the $q$-dimensional Weil
    representation of frequency $c \in (\Z/q\Z)^\times$.
    The admissible Dirichlet form on the admissible sector of $\Cq$ is
    \[
      \Eq(f,f) = q^{-2}\sum_{s \in S_q} a_q(s)\,\|\rho_c(s)f - f\|^2_{\Cq},
    \]
    with admissibility weights $a_q(s) > 0$ satisfying~[H-$w$]~\cite{Beau2026w1}.
    Throughout, $\exi{\xi}(k) = q^{-1/2} e^{2\pi i \xi k/q}$ denotes the normalised
    Fourier mode, $\|\exi{\xi}\|_{\Cq} = 1$.

  \subsection{Two projection objects}
    \label{subsec:two-projections}

    The O-series pipeline~\cite{Beau2026a29} stores two distinct objects per
    conjugate pair $(c, q-c)$:

    \begin{description}
      \item[\texttt{basis\_c[$p$]}] A unitary $(q \times q)$ matrix whose rows
      form a Gram--Schmidt orthonormal basis of $\C^q$ obtained by running
      BFS exploration up to saturation (99\% of $G_q$).
      This matrix spans all of $\C^q$ at saturation.

      \item[\texttt{pi\_c[$p$, $s$]}] An $(N_s \times 3)$ complex matrix of
      projection coordinates: each row gives the
      inner products $\langle \phi_j, v_i\rangle$ for $j = 0,1,2$, where
      $\phi_j$ are the first three GS basis vectors and $v_i$ runs over BFS
      fingerprints in shell $s$ of the fitting window $[n_0, n_1]$.
      The number of retained directions is fixed by the pipeline constant
      \texttt{HEFF\_DIM = 3} before any data are seen.
    \end{description}

    \begin{remark}[No canonical identification of $\Piq$ with the truncation]
      \label{rem:no-identification}
      The three-coordinate object identifies a rank-3 projection onto
      $\mathrm{span}\{\phi_0, \phi_1, \phi_2\}$ by \emph{convention}: the constant
      \texttt{HEFF\_DIM = 3} selects the number of directions in advance.
      No theorem of the programme identifies this truncation with a canonical admissible
      projection.
      The threshold $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ of the admissibility series is itself a supplied
      selection rule, not a derived constant~\cite{Beau2026a27}, and the canonical
      filtration stage of the fibre is the growing Fourier window of
      dimension $\min(2n+1,q)$ identified in~\cite{Beau2026q5a}, not a fixed rank-3
      subspace.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Pure Fourier modes in the pre-saturation pipeline}
  \label{sec:purity}

  \begin{proposition}[BFS fingerprints are pure modes]
    \label{prop:fingerprint-purity}
    For a group element $g$ reached by the BFS exploration and a block multi-index
    $\mathbf{c} = (c_1, c_2, c_3) \in ((\Z/q\Z)^{\times})^3$, the \emph{three-step BFS
    fingerprint} is the componentwise (Hadamard) product
    $v_{g,\mathbf{c}} = \bigl(\rho_{c_1}(g_1)\psi_0\bigr) \odot
    \bigl(\rho_{c_2}(g_2)\psi_0\bigr) \odot \bigl(\rho_{c_3}(g_3)\psi_0\bigr)$ of the
    three block images of the uniform initial vector $\psi_0 = \exi{0}$, where
    $g_1, g_2, g_3$ are the accumulated group elements of the three blocks.
    Every such fingerprint is a pure Fourier mode:
    \[
      v_{g,\mathbf{c}}(k) \propto e^{2\pi i \xi k/q},
      \qquad \xi = (c_1 b_1 + c_2 b_2 + c_3 b_3) \bmod q,
    \]
    where $b_j$ is the displacement of $g_j$ along the generator whose Weil action is the
    position-space modulation.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    In the Weil representation, one Heisenberg generator acts on functions on $\Z/q\Z$ by
    translation and the other by modulation; on the uniform vector the translation part
    contributes only a relabelling absorbed into a global phase, while a modulation
    displacement $b$ in block $j$ contributes the factor $e^{2\pi i c_j b k/q}$, a pure
    phase linear in $k$.
    The central generator contributes a global phase.
    Each block image $\rho_{c_j}(g_j)\psi_0$ is therefore proportional to a single Fourier
    mode of frequency $c_j b_j$, and the Hadamard product multiplies the phases, giving a
    single mode at the stated combined frequency.
    This is the O12 frequency formula~\cite{Beau2026a16}.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{proposition}[Gram--Schmidt preserves purity]
    \label{prop:gs-purity}
    In the pre-saturation window $[n_0, n_1]$ with $n_*(q) = o(q)$, the Gram--Schmidt
    basis vectors extracted from the BFS fingerprints are pure Fourier modes.
    In particular, the first three selected vectors $\phi_0, \phi_1, \phi_2$ are pure
    Fourier modes.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    By Proposition~\ref{prop:fingerprint-purity}, the fingerprints visiting the window
    are pure modes at frequencies in a finite set.
    Fourier modes at distinct frequencies are orthogonal in $\Cq$, so the Gram--Schmidt
    procedure either discards a fingerprint (repeated frequency) or normalises it
    (new frequency); it never mixes distinct frequencies.
    Every selected basis vector is therefore a single normalised mode.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{proposition}[Structure of the realised frequencies]
    \label{prop:small-frequencies}
    In the pre-saturation regime $n \le n_*(q) = o(q)$:
    (i) the realised displacements satisfy $|b_j| \le n_*(q) = o(q)$, with no modular
    wrapping;
    (ii) if the block parameters $(c_1, c_2, c_3)$ are held fixed as integers independent
    of $q$, every realised frequency satisfies
    $|\xi| \le (|c_1|+|c_2|+|c_3|)\, n_*(q) = o(q)$, so $\xi/q \to 0$;
    (iii) in the implemented pipeline, which samples the block parameters uniformly in
    $(\Z/q\Z)^{\times}$ at each $q$~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a29}, realised frequencies
    of macroscopic size $\xi/q = \Theta(1)$ occur (e.g.\ $c_1 = \lfloor q/2 \rfloor$,
    $b_1 = 1$), confined to $o(q)$-radius clusters around block-dependent residues.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    The displacements accessible within $n$ BFS steps satisfy $|b_j| \le n \le n_*(q)$,
    and $n_*(q) = o(q)$ excludes wrapping, giving (i).
    For (ii), $\xi$ is a fixed integer combination of the $b_j$ with $q$-independent
    coefficients, hence $o(q)$.
    For (iii), the frequency $\xi = \sum_j c_j b_j \bmod q$ with sampled residues $c_j$
    ranges over the displacement-shifted lattice of the sampled block; varying the $b_j$
    within the pre-saturation window moves $\xi$ by $o(q)$, which yields the cluster
    description, and the example realises $\xi/q$ arbitrarily close to $\tfrac12$.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[No $q$-independent macroscopic frequency]
    \label{rem:no-macroscopic}
    Proposition~\ref{prop:small-frequencies} separates two regimes that the pipeline
    literature has conflated.
    With fixed block parameters, the realised frequencies are $o(q)$ and no macroscopic
    limit exists except zero.
    With the implemented per-$q$ sampling, macroscopic frequencies occur at each $q$, but
    the block parameters are resampled as $q$ varies, so the pipeline defines no
    $q$-independent limit value $\omega_c \in (0,\tfrac12)$: a convergence statement
    $\xi_c^{(q)}/q \to \omega_c$ requires a $q$-uniform choice of blocks that the
    pipeline does not supply.
    Neither regime supports a fixed macroscopic frequency derived from the block
    structure alone.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Numerical claims]
    \label{rem:numerics}
    This paper makes no numerical support-size claims.
    The archived pipeline diagnostics for support sizes are not reproducible from the
    present repository (the required checkpoints are not versioned here), and they
    include a delocalised case (prime $q = 61$, pair $c = 2$) that is incompatible with a
    uniform single-mode support table.
    A reproducible support-size measurement, run on the full stored basis with a
    preregistered criterion, is left to future work.
  \end{remark}

  \section{The three-coordinate reduction is a truncation}
  \label{sec:truncation}

  \begin{proposition}[Rank cannot be measured from the truncated coordinates]
    \label{prop:truncation}
    Every quantity computed from the \texttt{pi\_c} arrays is a function of the
    orthogonal projections of the fingerprints onto
    $\mathrm{span}\{\phi_0, \phi_1, \phi_2\}$.
    In particular, any ``rank'' read from these arrays equals the number of retained
    coordinates fixed by \texttt{HEFF\_DIM} and carries no information about the
    dimension of the admissible sector beyond the first three selected directions.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    Immediate from the definition of the stored object: each row of \texttt{pi\_c} lists
    the inner products with the first three selected basis vectors only.
    Components of the fingerprints orthogonal to
    $\mathrm{span}\{\phi_0,\phi_1,\phi_2\}$ are not recorded, so no functional of
    \texttt{pi\_c} can detect them.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[What a rank measurement would require]
    \label{rem:rank-protocol}
    A measurement of the admissible rank must operate on the full stored basis
    (\texttt{basis\_c}), which reaches rank $q$ at saturation, with a preregistered,
    scale-stable rank criterion that does not fix the target dimension in advance.
    The exact filtration result of~\cite{Beau2026q5a} --- canonical stages of dimension
    $\min(2n+1, q)$ --- shows that the admissible carrier grows with the window and is
    not a fixed rank-3 subspace; any finite-rank reduction is an additional modelling
    step whose validity must be established separately.
  \end{remark}

  \section{The $q^{-1}$ coercivity scale is ruled out}
  \label{sec:coercivity}

  \begin{proposition}[Upper bound on the form at the $q^{-2}$ scale]
    \label{prop:no-coercivity}
    For every $f \in \Cq$ with $\|f\|_{\Cq} = 1$,
    \[
      \Eq(f, f)
      \;=\; q^{-2} \sum_{s \in S_q} a_q(s)\, \|\rho_c(s)f - f\|^2_{\Cq}
      \;\le\; 16\, a_{\max}\, q^{-2},
    \]
    where $a_{\max} = \sup_q \sup_s a_q(s) < \infty$ under~[H-$w$]~\cite{Beau2026w1}.
    Consequently there is no constant $c > 0$ independent of $q$ with
    $\Eq(f,f) \ge c\, q^{-1} \|f\|^2_{\Cq}$ on any non-trivial subspace: the scaled
    coercivity bound fails on every normalised vector as soon as $q > 16\,a_{\max}/c$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    Each $\rho_c(s)$ is unitary, so for every unit vector $f$,
    $\|(\rho_c(s) - I)f\| \le \|\rho_c(s)f\| + \|f\| = 2$, hence
    $\|(\rho_c(s) - I)f\|^2 \le 4$.
    Summing the four generator terms gives the stated bound with constant
    $4 \times 4 = 16$, and $16\,a_{\max}\,q^{-2} < c\,q^{-1}$ for all
    $q > 16\,a_{\max}/c$.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Scope: a normalisation fact, and the untouched spectral question]
    \label{rem:zero-form}
    Proposition~\ref{prop:no-coercivity} uses only unitarity and the $q^{-2}$
    normalisation prefactor in the definition of $\Eq$; it holds on the whole unit
    sphere and is therefore a statement about the form's normalisation convention, not
    about the spectrum of any particular subspace.
    It is the pipeline-level face of the exact result of~\cite{Beau2026q5a}: on the
    canonical Fourier-window filtration the published admissibility form converges to
    the zero form, uniformly on norm-bounded subsets, and its Mosco limit is the zero
    form.
    The substantive spectral question --- a gap for the unnormalised Dirichlet form, or
    for a renormalised form on the canonical filtration --- is untouched by this
    proposition and remains open.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[No compactness route through plane waves]
    \label{rem:no-plane-waves}
    An embedding of the finite modes $\exi{\xi}$ as plane waves
    $e^{2\pi i \omega x}$ on the line cannot underlie a strong $L^2(\R)$ compactness
    argument: plane waves are not elements of $L^2(\R)$.
    Any continuum comparison must specify an isometric embedding with normalisable
    images (for instance, windowed or weighted modes); the disposition of the continuum
    limit on the canonical filtration is the one established in~\cite{Beau2026q5a}.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Discussion and outlook}
  \label{sec:discussion}

  The pre-saturation pipeline has an exact and simple Fourier description: fingerprints
  and selected Gram--Schmidt vectors are pure modes, with $o(q)$ displacements and
  block-dependent frequencies (macroscopic per-$q$ values under the implemented sampling,
  with no $q$-independent limit).
  This structure is a property of the fingerprint construction and of Gram--Schmidt
  orthogonality; it holds independently of any rank hypothesis.

  The three-coordinate reduction of the pipeline is a truncation.
  Its outputs live in a subspace fixed in advance, so they can characterise the first
  three selected directions but cannot measure the dimension of the admissible sector.
  The canonical object behind the pipeline is the growing Fourier-window filtration
  identified in~\cite{Beau2026q5a}, on which the published admissibility form has
  zero-form Mosco limit; the analytic programme of the original Q5a framework is to be
  pursued on that filtration, not through a fixed finite rank.

  Three items are open.
  First, a reproducible support-size measurement on the full stored basis, with a
  preregistered criterion (Remark~\ref{rem:numerics}).
  Second, a rank-measurement protocol that does not fix the target dimension in advance
  (Remark~\ref{rem:rank-protocol}).
  Third, hypothesis [H2] of~\cite{Beau2026q5a} --- strong convergence of the rescaled
  generators --- which is untouched by the present results.
